Lost Luggage, Frozen Hands & Unexpected Kindness

My journey to Poland began with a little language confusion.

An elderly Polish woman approached me and asked for help with her luggage. After I helped her, she started chatting away in Polish. I had absolutely no idea what she was saying, so I did what seemed most appropriate: smiled and nodded along.

A promising start, wasn’t it?

Welcome to Warsaw. Where’s My Bag?

I arrived at Warsaw Central Station around 6:30 in the morning.

I left one of my bags unattended for a minute while I went to the station information office. When I returned, it was gone.

Just like that.

I approached the station officers for help, but unfortunately, I couldn’t recover it.

Of course, I could have been more careful. I knew that. But standing there, I also realised something: I had two choices. I could spend the rest of the trip feeling miserable about what had happened, or I could accept it and make the most of being somewhere new.

So I told myself, It’s not the end of the world. Deal with it, and enjoy your trip.

After all, travelling isn’t just about how much money you spend or how perfectly everything goes. Sometimes, it’s about having the courage to keep going when things don’t work out.

Frozen Hands and Warm Drinks

Warsaw welcomed me with temperatures around -5°C.

I wasn’t particularly thrilled about that part.

My hands were freezing, and I quickly developed a deep appreciation for hot chocolate and coffee. Sometimes, holding a warm cup felt like the most important activity of the day.

I stayed with Ewa, a surgeon, and her dog, Tajga.

Through Ewa, I learned more about Warsaw and its history. I also had the opportunity to experience everyday life beyond the usual tourist attractions.

And, of course, there was the food.

A Little Taste of Poland

One of my favourite things about travelling is discovering what people eat.

In Poland, I tried traditional dishes including bigos, makowiec, żurek served in bread, stuffed fish, pyzy and pierogi.

Every dish offered something different, and I enjoyed discovering flavours I hadn’t experienced before.

I also learned to bake Polish bread and cookies, which made the experience even more memorable.

Between cooking, exploring and spending time playing with the dogs, I began to feel more connected to the place.

That’s something I love about travelling. The small, everyday moments often stay with me longer than the famous attractions.

Strangers Who Made the Journey Special

One evening, I was confused about the ticket system and approached a girl named Ola for help.

She was friendly and happy to explain things. As it turned out, we were both getting off at the same stop, so we ended up having a lovely little conversation along the way.

Later, when I wanted to visit a salon, I met Anna.

She offered me a drink, and we started talking about Indian food. She told me she enjoyed spicy dishes, which gave us an immediate topic to bond over.

These weren’t extraordinary encounters. They were simply people being kind, curious and willing to connect.

But those are often the moments that make a trip feel special.

When it was time for me to return to Berlin, Ewa kindly dropped me off at Warsaw station.

I had arrived in Poland with an unexpected problem. I left with memories of people who had made me feel welcome.

What Poland Taught Me

I think every destination has something interesting to offer, even when the journey doesn’t go as planned.

Losing my bag wasn’t pleasant. Neither was walking around with frozen hands. But neither experience defined my time in Poland.

Instead, I remember the conversations, the food, the warmth of Ewa’s home, and the kindness of people I met along the way.

Perhaps I’m naturally optimistic, but I believe there’s something worth discovering wherever we go.

And sometimes, the best thing we can bring home isn’t a souvenir.

It’s a reminder that, despite our different languages, cultures and backgrounds, people everywhere appreciate the same simple things: kindness, connection and a little happiness.

That was my Warsaw experience. Not perfect, but definitely memorable.
