2019

Becoming Someone New Without Losing Yourself

I still remember how excited I was about moving to Germany.

It wasn’t a decision that happened overnight. It took time to convince my family back home, and there were plenty of conversations before everything finally came together.

I’d received admission offers from two universities in Germany. Something I’d been working towards was finally becoming real.

I was excited about studying abroad, discovering a new country and experiencing a completely different way of life.

But was I nervous?

Absolutely.

I was moving somewhere I didn’t know anyone. Everything familiar—my family, my language, my routines, the comfort of knowing how things worked—was about to change.

And although I knew that, I don’t think I fully understood what starting over would feel like until I actually did it.

When the Smallest Things Become Challenges

The first few weeks in Germany were quite an experience.

Back home in India, I’d grown up in an environment where I was looked after and supported. I was used to having people around who could help when something went wrong.

Suddenly, I had to figure things out myself.

Even something as ordinary as opening a bank account took around two weeks.

Travelling by train could be confusing. I remember an incident during my first week when someone was shouting in German, and I couldn’t fully understand what was happening around me.

There were unfamiliar rules, new systems and a language I was still learning.

I’d made an effort to learn some basic German before moving, but knowing a few words and navigating everyday life are two very different things.

Things I’d never thought twice about suddenly required planning, patience and courage.

And sometimes, I was scared.

Looking back, I realise that independence doesn’t always arrive as some big, exciting moment.

Sometimes, it’s simply waking up the next morning and figuring out the next thing.

Finding My Own Way

My initial living situation wasn’t easy.

There were difficult experiences that made me uncomfortable, and for a while, I had to deal with an environment that didn’t feel right for me.

But eventually, I found a way out.

I was fortunate to move into a beautiful, peaceful home with a German-Indian family. The house was spacious, the atmosphere was welcoming, and for the first time in a while, I felt I could settle in.

That move meant more to me than simply finding a better place to live.

It reminded me that I had choices.

I could recognise when something wasn’t working, make a decision and create a better situation for myself.

It wasn’t always comfortable, but it was an important step towards becoming more independent.

Discovering India While Living in Germany

Something unexpected happened after I moved abroad.

I began appreciating India in a completely different way.

India is incredibly diverse. We have different languages, cultures, traditions and food, sometimes changing completely from one state to another.

Of course, I’d always known that.

But living in Germany made me experience that diversity differently.

The Indian members of the family I lived with came from a different part of India. We didn’t share the same regional language or cultural background.

Yet there was still that familiar connection.

We had things in common, but we also had so much to learn about each other.

It was a lovely reminder that home isn’t always one language, one tradition or one particular way of living.

Sometimes, being away from your country helps you understand it better.

And I found myself feeling grateful for where I came from, even as I was learning to build a life somewhere new.

The People Who Made a New Place Feel Familiar

While living with the family, I helped with things around the house and also began earning some money.

Gradually, I met more people.

Some encounters were brief. Others developed into friendships.

What makes me smile today is that I’m still in contact with some of the people I met during that period.

At the time, I didn’t know how important those connections would become.

They were simply people I met while trying to find my feet in a new country.

But looking back, they became part of the reason Germany started feeling more familiar.

I learned that belonging doesn’t necessarily happen the moment you arrive somewhere.

Sometimes, it grows slowly through conversations, shared experiences, small acts of kindness and friendships you never expected to make.

You don’t have to know everyone when you arrive.

Sometimes, you meet the right people along the way.

Becoming More Confident, Not Fearless

When I think about the person I was when I first moved to Germany, I remember someone who was excited, curious and also quite scared.

And when I compare her with the person I am today, I can see how much has changed.

I’m more confident about making decisions.

I’m more comfortable handling unfamiliar situations.

And I’ve learned that I can find my way through circumstances that initially seem overwhelming.

But here’s something I want to be clear about.

I’m not fearless now.

There are still things that scare me. There are still moments when I doubt myself or feel uncertain.

The difference is that I have more trust in my ability to deal with them.

I can look back at the younger version of myself and think, You were scared back then, too. But you still did it.

And perhaps that’s what confidence really is.

Not the absence of fear, but knowing that fear doesn’t always have to decide what happens next.

The Parts of Me That Never Changed

Moving to another country changed many things about my life.

I became more independent, learned to navigate unfamiliar situations and grew more comfortable making decisions for myself.

But some parts of me stayed exactly the same.

My curiosity.

My optimism.

My belief in kindness.

I’ve always been curious about people, cultures and different ways of living. Moving abroad gave me more opportunities to explore those interests.

And even when things were difficult, I tried to remain optimistic and open to the people around me.

If anything, living away from home made those qualities more meaningful.

I learned that becoming a different version of yourself doesn’t require abandoning the person you used to be.

You can become stronger without becoming harder.

You can become independent and still value connection.

You can embrace a new culture while becoming even more grateful for your own.

Growth doesn’t always mean replacing who you were.

Sometimes, it simply means discovering more of who you’ve always been.

A Little Note to My Younger Self

If I could meet the younger version of myself, the one who was preparing to leave India for Germany, I think I’d recognise that mixture of excitement and nervousness immediately.

She’d have so many questions.

Would everything work out?

Would she find good people?

Would she manage on her own?

Would she ever feel at home somewhere so unfamiliar?

I wouldn’t promise her that everything would be easy.

Because it wasn’t.

There would be uncomfortable moments, difficult decisions, unexpected kindness and friendships that would last for years.

There would be times when she’d feel confident and others when she’d wonder whether she’d made the right choice.

But through all of it, she would keep learning.

And she would find her own way.

So instead of giving her a long list of advice, I’d simply tell her:

You got this. I believe in you. And I’m here for you.

Because sometimes, growing up isn’t about becoming someone completely new.

It’s about learning to trust yourself, appreciating where you came from and discovering that you can build a life in a new place without losing the things that make you who you are.

And I’m still learning.

Perhaps that’s the beautiful part of the journey within. It never really ends.
